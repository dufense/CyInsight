Below are reusable, neutral, and concise email templates an AI SOC analyst can use for different attack types. You can parameterize fields like [Ticket_ID], [Hostname], [User_ID], [Severity], [Attack_Type], etc., in your tool.

1. Generic incident notification (any attack type)
Subject: [SEV-[Severity]] [Attack_Type] detected on [System/User] – Ticket [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [Recipient_Group],

This is to inform you that a [Attack_Type] has been detected on [System/User] at [Detection_Time] by our security monitoring systems. The incident has been logged under ticket [Ticket_ID] and is currently under investigation by the SOC team.
​

Initial details:

Affected asset(s): [Hostname/IP/Application/User]

Detection source: [EDR/SIEM/Email Gateway/WAF/etc.]

Severity: [Critical/High/Medium/Low]

Current status: [New/Investigating/Contained/Monitoring]

Immediate actions requested from you (if any):

[E.g., Do not use the affected system until cleared, reset password, confirm business impact, validate recent changes]

We will share further updates, including root cause, impact, and remediation steps, as investigation progresses or if your immediate action is required.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]
[SOC / Cybersecurity Team]

2. Malware infection (endpoint/server)
Subject: Malware activity detected on [Hostname] – Action required – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [IT_Ops/Owner],

Our monitoring tools have detected malware-related activity on the following asset:

Hostname: [Hostname]

IP: [IP]

User: [User_ID]

Detection time: [Time]

Malware family / signature: [If available]

Detection source: [EDR/AV/SIEM]

Current SOC actions:

Isolated host from the network: [Yes/No]

Malicious file(s) quarantined or blocked: [Yes/No/Details]

Indicators of compromise (IOCs) identified: [File path, hash, domain, IP]

Required actions from you:

Validate if the asset can be taken offline for full scan and remediation.

Run full AV/EDR scan and remove/quarantine all detections.

Confirm any business impact or critical processes running on this host.

We will update this ticket once containment and eradication steps are completed and will provide a final incident report.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]

3. Phishing attack (user notification / awareness)
Subject: Security Alert: Phishing email detected – Please delete

Body:
Dear [User/All_Employees],

We have identified that a phishing email with the subject "[Phishing_Subject]" was delivered to some user mailboxes on [Date/Time]. The email contains malicious content designed to steal credentials or deliver malware.

What you should do:

If you received this email, do not click any links or open attachments.

Delete the email from Inbox and Deleted Items.

If you already clicked a link or entered any credentials, immediately change your password and inform the IT/SOC team.

Key details:

Sender: [Sender_Address]

Subject: [Phishing_Subject]

Malicious indicator(s): [URL/Attachment name]

Thank you for your vigilance. Reporting suspicious emails helps protect the organization.

Regards,
[SOC / IT Security Team]

4. Account brute-force / credential stuffing
Subject: Multiple failed login attempts detected on account [User_ID] – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [IT_Ops/HR/Manager/User],

The SOC has detected a high volume of failed login attempts against the following account:
​

Account: [User_ID or Service_Account]

Target system: [AD/Email/VPN/Application]

Source IP(s): [IP_List]

Time window: [Start_Time – End_Time]

Status: [Account locked/Not locked]

SOC actions taken:

Enabled/confirmed account lockout: [Yes/No]

Blocked offending IP(s)/countries at [Firewall/WAF/VPN]: [Yes/No]

Monitoring for further attempts: [Yes/No]

Required actions:

Confirm if this login activity could be legitimate (e.g., misconfigured script, VPN issue).

If suspicious, reset the account password and enforce MFA (if not already enabled).

Validate any recent access from unusual locations with the user.

A final update and recommendations will follow once investigation is complete.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]

5. Ransomware incident
Subject: Critical: Suspected ransomware activity on [Environment] – Immediate response – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [CISO/IT_Head/Incident_Command],

We have identified indicators consistent with a ransomware attack affecting [Hosts/Environment].

Current observations:

Affected systems: [Hostnames/Business apps]

Detection time: [Time]

Symptoms: [File encryption, ransom note, unusual extensions, services stopped]

Ransom note file name/location (if present): [Details]

SOC actions initiated:

Isolating affected systems from the network.

Blocking known malicious IPs/domains associated with the incident.

Preserving forensic evidence (logs, memory, disk snapshots) where possible.

Immediate requests:

Authorize isolation of additional at-risk systems if needed.

Activate the incident response / crisis management plan and relevant communication channels.

Confirm legal, PR, and compliance contacts for regulatory notifications (if data exposure is suspected).

We will continue to provide structured updates, including containment status, recovery strategy, and business impact assessment.

Regards,
[SOC Incident Handler]

6. Network intrusion / lateral movement
Subject: Suspicious network activity and possible intrusion on [Segment] – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [Network_Team/IT_Ops],

The SOC has detected suspicious network activity indicative of possible unauthorized access or lateral movement.

Details:

Affected segment/VLAN: [Segment]

Key hosts involved: [IPs/Hostnames]

Detected behavior: [Port scanning, lateral RDP, SMB enumeration, data exfiltration patterns]

Detection source: [IDS/IPS/NDR/SIEM]

Timeframe: [Start_Time – Ongoing]

SOC actions:

Applied temporary blocks or ACLs on suspicious IPs/ports.

Enabled increased logging and packet capture on affected segments.

Correlated events with endpoint and identity logs for potential compromise.

Requested actions:

Validate whether any of this traffic is expected (e.g., new tool, maintenance).

Review recent changes and privileged sessions on the involved systems.

Assist with additional segmentation or isolation as required.

We will escalate severity and notify additional stakeholders if confirmed compromise is identified.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]

7. Web application attack (OWASP Top 10 style)
Subject: Web application attack detected on [App_Name] – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [App_Owner/DevOps],

Our monitoring has detected suspicious web traffic against the application [App_Name] that aligns with known OWASP Top 10 patterns (e.g., injection, authentication abuse, XSS).
​

Summary:

Application: [URL / App_Name]

Attack type(s): [SQL injection / XSS / Broken Auth / Path traversal / etc.]

Source IP(s): [IPs or Geo]

Detection source: [WAF/SIEM/IDS]

Timeframe: [Start_Time – Ongoing]

SOC actions:

Blocked or rate-limited malicious IPs/ranges at WAF or firewall.

Enabled or tightened specific WAF rules for the relevant attack pattern.

Captured HTTP request samples for forensic and development review.

Required actions:

Review the captured requests and assess if any exploit attempts were successful.

Validate that input validation, authentication, and authorization controls are correctly implemented and patched.

Plan remediation (code fix/config change) and regression testing if vulnerabilities are confirmed.

We will share additional IOCs and recommendations as more data is analyzed.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]

8. Vulnerability exploit attempt / exploit confirmed
Subject: Exploit attempt against [Vulnerability_ID] on [System] – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [System_Owner/IT_Ops],

We have detected activity indicating an attempt to exploit vulnerability [CVE-ID / Vendor_Bulletin] on the following system(s):

Asset: [Hostname/IP]

Vulnerability: [CVE-ID, short description]

Detection time: [Time]

Detection source: [IDS/WAF/EDR/SIEM]

Status: [Attempted/Confirmed successful/Under investigation]

SOC actions:

Blocked the offending IP(s)/request patterns where feasible.

Checked for known post-exploitation indicators on the host(s).

Correlated with vulnerability scanning data to confirm exposure.

Required actions:

Apply the recommended patch/workaround for [Vulnerability_ID] on all affected systems as a priority.

Validate system integrity (logs, processes, services, scheduled tasks) for signs of compromise.

Confirm maintenance window and rollback plans if needed.

We will provide a follow-up report summarizing whether the exploit attempt was successful and any remediation taken.

Regards,
[SOC Analyst Name]

9. Social engineering (vishing, smishing, in-person, business email compromise)
Subject: Security Alert: Social engineering attempt reported – [Ticket_ID]

Body:
Dear [HR/All_Employees/Specific_User],

The SOC has received a report of a social engineering attempt targeting [User/Department] on [Date/Time].

Incident details:

Type: [Phishing / Vishing / Smishing / In-person / BEC]

Channel: [Email/Phone/SMS/Physical]

Attacker pretext: [e.g., pretending to be IT support, vendor, senior executive]

Requested information/actions: [Passwords, OTP, payment, confidential data]

SOC actions:

Validated that the communication is not from a legitimate internal or trusted external source.

Blocked any related domains/addresses/phone numbers where technically possible.

Logged IOCs and updated awareness material.

Required actions and awareness:

Do not share passwords, OTPs, or financial information with anyone claiming to be from internal teams over email/SMS/unsolicited calls.

Report any similar attempts immediately to SOC/IT security.

If any sensitive information was shared, notify SOC so that we can initiate containment (password reset, access review, etc.).

Regards,
[SOC / Security Awareness Team]

10. Final incident closure / executive summary
Subject: Incident [Ticket_ID] – [Attack_Type] – Investigation closed

Body:
Dear [Stakeholders/Management],

This email summarizes the closure of incident [Ticket_ID] related to [Attack_Type] initially detected on [Date/Time].
​

What happened

Brief summary of the incident: [2–3 lines, business-language description]

Affected systems/users: [List]

Detection source: [Tool/Process]

Impact

Business impact: [Downtime, data exposure (if any), affected services]

Scope: [Number of systems/users/sites impacted]

Root cause

Root cause identified: [Misconfiguration, missing patch, user action, third-party compromise, etc.]

Actions taken

Containment: [Steps]

Eradication and recovery: [Rebuild, patching, password resets, etc.]

Lessons learned & recommendations

Process/technology improvements: [Monitoring rules, playbooks, hardening]

User awareness or policy changes: [Training, revised procedures]

The incident is now marked as Closed, with continued monitoring in place for any recurrence.

Regards,
[SOC Manager / Incident Commander]

11. Suggested structure for your AI SOC analyst
For your tool, define a standard JSON structure like:

json
{
  "attack_type": "malware | phishing | brute_force | ransomware | owasp | vuln_exploit | network_intrusion | social_engineering | generic",
  "audience": "user | it_ops | management | all_employees",
  "severity": "Low | Medium | High | Critical",
  "placeholders": {
    "Ticket_ID": "",
    "Hostname": "",
    "IP": "",
    "User_ID": "",
    "Detection_Time": "",
    "Tool": "",
    "CVE_ID": "",
    "App_Name": "",
    "Phishing_Subject": ""
  },
  "email": {
    "subject_template": "...",
    "body_template": "..."
  }
}
Your AI SOC analyst can then select the appropriate attack_type and audience, fill in placeholders from SIEM/EDR data, and generate clean incident emails consistently.
